# Outreach playbook

How every cold email and DM is written. Read `profile/me.md` and `profile/facts.md` first.

## Rules
1. **Short.** Under ~150 words in the body. Founders read on their phone.
2. **Human.** Write like a person who typed it quickly and means it. No "I hope this email finds you well", no buzzword lists, no em dashes.
3. **About them first.** Open with their product or their own words (a founder post, a careers page line, a paper). Never open with "I".
4. **One or two proofs, tied to their problem.** Not a skills list. Outcomes in plain words, not numbers a non-engineer can't read.
5. **One honest personal line.** From the profile's story section. Real, not dramatic.
6. **One way you'd help.** One plain sentence tied to their pain. Different ideas for competing companies; never send rivals the same pitch.
7. **Simple ask.** A 15-minute call, or a small paid trial task. Never "please find my resume attached for the position".
8. **Honest.** Nothing that isn't in `profile/facts.md`. Learning something? Say "learning", never "expert". Placeholders like `[result]` for results that don't exist yet.

## Email structure
```
Subject: <specific, about them> (or the exact role name)

tldr; <1 to 2 true, personal lines: who you are + what you want to do there>

Hi <first name>,

<Their product and the problem it solves, ideally in their words. Congrats on the raise/launch.>

<The thing you built that is closest to their problem, as an outcome. One line of recent proof.>

<How you'd help them, one plain sentence.>

<Ask: quick call or small trial task. Location/visa line if abroad.>

Best,
<name>
<one link>
```

## LinkedIn / X note (under 300 characters)
Their thing in one line + your closest proof in one line + a soft ask. No links if the platform penalises them; put the link in a reply or second message.

## Before showing a draft, check
- [ ] The hook uses their own words, with a source.
- [ ] Every claim about the user is in `profile/facts.md`.
- [ ] Nothing from the profile's "Private" section.
- [ ] Under the word limit. No em or en dashes.
- [ ] Right resume from the profile's table.
- [ ] The email address is verified, or clearly labelled a guess.

## Follow-ups
- Follow-up 1 after 5 to 7 days: 2 lines, plus one new thing (a small idea, a relevant post, a demo).
- Follow-up 2 after 7 more days: one line, close politely.
- Then stop and move on.

## What tends to fail (from real use)
- Generic inboxes with generic emails get no reply.
- Too technical for founders. Translate results into what they mean for the user.
- The same formula every time ("One idea:"). Vary it.
- Judging too early: give it a week before changing the template.
